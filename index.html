10 High-Level Idioms (with Romanian Equivalents)

Since you already know standard vocabulary, let's upgrade to Cambridge B2 & Olympiad idioms. Study these 10 figurative expressions, their Romanian translations, and how native speakers use them:

1. Cut corners
RO: A face treabă de mântuială / A scurta drumul
"If you cut corners on the Technic gearbox, the wheels will jam later."
2. Back to the drawing board
RO: De la capăt / O luăm de la zero
"The bridge collapsed during testing, so it's back to the drawing board."
3. A blessing in disguise
RO: Un rău spre bine
"Losing the grey bricks was a blessing in disguise because the black ones looked cooler."
4. Bite off more than you can chew
RO: A se înhăma la mai mult decât poate duce
"Building a 6,000-piece set in one evening meant biting off more than I could chew."
5. Hit the nail on the head
RO: A pune punctul pe „i” / A nimeri exact
"Theo hit the nail on the head when he spotted the reversed axle."
6. Cost an arm and a leg
RO: A costa o avere / O căruță de bani
"Collector's edition LEGO sets usually cost an arm and a leg."
7. Once in a blue moon
RO: Din an în Paște / Foarte rar
"Our robotics club enters an international contest once in a blue moon."
8. Call it a day
RO: A încheia munca pe ziua de azi
"We've finished 400 steps—let's call it a day and continue tomorrow."
9. Get out of hand
RO: A scăpa de sub control
"When everyone dumped their brick boxes onto the floor, things got out of hand."
10. On the same page
RO: Pe aceeași lungime de undă
"Before we build the tower, let's make sure we are all on the same page."

Missing Sentence Logic: "The Dragon Core Mystery"

In Cambridge B2 First (Part 6), full sentences have been removed from the middle of a story. Read the sentences before and after each gap carefully to figure out which missing sentence (A–E) connects the logic smoothly. (There is one extra sentence you do not need!)

Every spring, thousands of young builders travel to the Ninjago Brick Convention to display their custom creations. Last April, fourteen-year-old Leo spent four months assembling a giant mechanical Fire Dragon out of 8,000 red and gold LEGO pieces. [GAP 1] Whenever spectators clapped their hands near the table, the dragon roared and spread its wings.

On the morning of the final judging, however, Leo arrived at the hall and realized the dragon's wings were completely frozen. [GAP 2] Security guards immediately checked the main exit cameras, yet nobody had left the building carrying a suspicious bag.

While everyone else panicked, Leo remembered that his smart battery connected via Bluetooth to an app on his phone. [GAP 3] Following the green radar dot on his screen, Leo walked across the convention floor to a snack booth twenty meters away.

There, sitting beside a stroller, was a mischievous toddler happily pressing the glowing buttons on the battery pack like a toy remote control. [GAP 4] Within two minutes, the dragon was roaring again, and the judges awarded Leo first place for both engineering and problem-solving.
